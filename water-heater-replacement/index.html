How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Age is the first clue. A tank past eight to twelve years has done most of its work, and the serial number on the label tells us exactly how old yours is. Then look at the symptoms. Water leaking from the tank, rust showing up only in the hot water, and heavy sediment noise all say the tank is close to finished. Parts like thermocouples, elements, and relief valves can be replaced on almost any heater, but a rusted-through tank cannot, and plenty of households in Elk Grove, CA usually find that out the hard way, with a wet floor.

A new water heater should fit the way your household uses hot water today, not the way it did when the old one went in. Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity
  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • A second or third repair within a year or two

Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and there is a lot to get right. Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
